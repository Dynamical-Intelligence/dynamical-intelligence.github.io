On June 30th 2026, **Zorka Bozilovic**, a Computer Science and Mathematics student at Queen Mary University of London, was honored with the Queen Mary School of Electronic Engineering and Computer Science Student Award for her outstanding undergraduate thesis on decoding music directly from brainwaves.

Working in the lab for the past year, Zorka set out to answer the question: can a machine "hear" the melody in someone's head by reading their EEG? Her thesis, [_"Decoding Melodic Acoustic Features from Neural Data"_](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~iran/papers/Bozilovic_and_Roman_AESAIMLA_2025.pdf), used machine learning to extract acoustic envelopes and note onsets straight from raw brain signals.

She found that decoding accuracy was consistently higher for trained musicians than for non-musicians, with the sharpest gains appearing in the brain's theta-band activity — the neural rhythm linked to memory and auditory processing. The findings suggest that musical training doesn't just change how we play music, it reshapes how our brains represent it.

Beyond the numbers, the work points toward real applications: smarter neural audio engineering tools, assistive technologies for communication, and rehabilitation devices that respond to how the brain processes sound.

All lab members are thrilled to see Zorka's dedication recognzsed on this stage. Her year in the lab combined rigorous EEG analysis, hands-on machine learning, and the kind of curiosity that turns a thesis into a genuine contribution to neuroscience. Our lab at Queen Mary University of London continues to be a place where undergraduate research pushes real scientific boundaries, and Zorka's award is a testament to that.

Congratulations, Zorka! We are all proud of you!
