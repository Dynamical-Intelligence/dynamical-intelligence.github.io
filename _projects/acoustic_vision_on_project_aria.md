Acoustic imaging has traditionally been used in industrial and engineering applications, for tasks such as locating noise sources, diagnosing machinery, and visualising otherwise invisible acoustic phenomena. This project explores how the role of acoustic imaging could expand beyond specialised measurement tools when brought into a personal, wearable device.

<img src="/assets/img/projects/aria.png" alt="Minifusion plugin" style="width: 50%; height: auto; margin-bottom: 1rem; display: block; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;">

Meta’s Project Aria is a research platform in the form of smart glasses equipped with a range of sensors, including cameras and a multi-channel microphone array. As academic collaborators supported through Meta Reality Labs, we investigate how sounds in the surrounding environment can be spatially localised and visualised in real time. Rather than relying on large, fixed microphone arrays and controlled measurement settings, the project explores acoustic imaging from the perspective of a wearable device that moves with the user and experiences the acoustic environment alongside them.

Ultimately, the project asks what new information and experiences could become possible when wearable devices allow users not only to hear the world around them, but also to perceive and understand its spatial acoustic structure.
